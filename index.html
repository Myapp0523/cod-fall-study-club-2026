Course Overview

Digital workflow and the Future of 3D Printing in Pediatric Dentistry and Application of AI with Review of Pediatric Crowns

This invited course explores how digital technology is changing pediatric dental care through intraoral scanning, milling, 3D printing and artificial intelligence, followed by a practical review of pediatric crown options. The program addresses clinical applications, benefits and limitations of digital workflows, responsible use of AI, and selection of restorative materials and crowns for pediatric patients.

Digital Workflow – Intraoral Scanning, Milling, and 3D Printing in Pediatric Dentistry

Dentistry has evolved with advances in materials and digital technology. This section focuses on the use of scanning, milling and 3D printing in pediatric dentistry and examines benefits, applications and limitations. Clinical applications include crowns, dentures, care for patients with special health care needs, presurgical nasoalveolar molding for cleft lip and palate, cancer, amelogenesis imperfecta, maxillofacial abnormalities and molar-incisor hypomineralization.

Learning Objectives

  • Learn how to digitize teeth, impressions and dentures using an intraoral scanner.
  • Understand the benefits and limitations of digital dentistry.
  • Learn how milling and 3D printing can benefit pediatric patients.

The Future of Pediatric Dentistry Utilizing AI in the Digital Era

This section reviews artificial intelligence in pediatric dentistry, including image-based tasks, tooth detection, caries identification and radiographic interpretation. It also addresses current limitations in clinical reasoning and treatment planning, along with fairness, ethics, governance, dataset diversity, bias, oversight, workflow integration and clinician readiness. AI is presented as a supportive clinical aid rather than a replacement for professional judgment.

The Good, The Bad, and The Ugly of Pediatric Crowns

A practical review of pediatric crown options, including stainless steel crowns, open-face stainless steel crowns, strip crowns, hybrid resin ceramic crowns, pre-veneered crowns, zirconia crowns and other restorative choices.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the different pediatric crowns available.
  • Become familiar with their strengths and weaknesses.
  • Recognize indications and contraindications for different crown options.

About Dr. Chen

Dr. Jung-Wei “Anna” Chen earned her DDS from Taipei Medical University in Taiwan in 1996. At the University of Maryland, Baltimore, she completed a Certificate in Pediatric Dentistry and a Master’s degree in Oral Biology in 2000. She later earned a Master’s degree in 2002 and a doctoral degree in 2007 in Health Informatics from The University of Texas Health Science Center at Houston, School of Health Information Science.

Dr. Chen joined Loma Linda University School of Dentistry as a full-time Associate Professor in 2008 and became Professor in 2014. She has served as Program Director of the Advanced Specialty Education Program in Pediatric Dentistry since 2009 and became Chair of the Department of Pediatric Dentistry in 2023.

She is a board-certified Diplomate and Board Examiner and is a member of the College of Diplomates of the American Board of Pediatric Dentistry. Her professional service has included leadership as Past President of the Pediatric Oral Health Research Group of the International Association for Dental Research, service with the California Society of Pediatric Dentistry, American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry committees, and the International Association of Paediatric Dentistry education committee.

Dr. Chen is a Fellow of the American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry, American College of Dentists, International College of Dentists, Academy of Dentistry International and International Association of Dental Traumatology. Her research interests include pediatric esthetic crowns, dental materials, cleft lip and palate, fluoride, HIV oral manifestations, sedation outcomes, dental trauma, pedagogy, digital dentistry, app development and artificial intelligence clinical research. She also maintains extramural pediatric private practice.
